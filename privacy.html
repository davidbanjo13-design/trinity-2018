What I do with your details

No legal fog. This is a reunion for one year group, run by one person from that year group, and here is exactly what happens to what you type into the form.

Who's asking

David, who was in Year 11 at Trinity School Bexley in 2018. Not the school, not a company. The school is not involved in running this and does not get your details.

What I collect

  • Your first and last name — so I know who's coming.
  • Your email address — so I can tell you the date once it's locked.
  • Your memory, if you write one — optional, and you can leave it blank.
  • Confirmation you were in the year group — this is only for our year.

That's everything. No phone number, no address, no date of birth, no payment details. I don't use cookies, I don't track you around the internet, and there's no Facebook or Google tracking on this site.

What I do with it

I email you about this reunion. That is the entire purpose. I will not sell it, share it, hand it to the school, or use it to sell you anything unrelated.

The legal basis is your consent — you chose to type it in — and you can withdraw that at any time by asking me to remove you.

The memories wall

If you write a memory, it only appears publicly if I approve it first. Nothing goes up automatically. When it does appear, it shows as your first name and last initial — "Jordan O." — never your full surname and never your email address.

If you'd rather your memory came down, say so and it's gone the same day. You stay signed up for the reunion either way.

Where it's kept, and for how long

In a database hosted by Supabase on servers in London. It's protected so that the public website can add a signup but cannot read the list back — nobody can pull the names and emails out of the page.

I'll keep it until the reunion has happened and then for a reasonable while after, in case we do it again. If you want out before then, just ask.

What you can ask me to do

You can ask me to:

  • tell you what I hold about you
  • correct it if it's wrong
  • delete it entirely
  • take your memory off the wall
  • stop emailing you

Email [ reunion email address — not set yet ] and I'll do it. No form, no process, no questions.

If I ever got this wrong and you weren't happy with how I handled it, you can complain to the UK's Information Commissioner at ico.org.uk.

Counting visits

I record anonymous counts — a page was viewed, a form was started, a form was completed — so I can tell whether this is working. No IP address, no cookie, nothing stored on your device, and nothing that links a visit to you or your signup.
